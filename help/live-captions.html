    Screens and livestream

    Live captions

    Live captions turn speech from a microphone or a video playing on the congregation screen into words along the bottom of that screen: the sermon, the notices, the prayers. They help people who are hard of hearing, and anyone sitting where the sound is poor.

    The speech is recognised on this computer. Nothing that's said is sent anywhere or kept.

    Live captions currently recognise English speech only.

    Turning them on

    1. In the right-hand column, open Live captions. (The first time, tick it under + More tools at the bottom of the column.)
    2. Under Caption accuracy, choose Fast for words as they are spoken, or More accurate for steadier words several seconds later.
    3. Under Sound from, choose Microphone or sound desk (see below) or Video playing on congregation screen.
    4. Tick On.

    Each mode downloads its speech model once: about 100 MB from GitHub for Fast, or about 375 MB from Hugging Face for More accurate. A bar shows progress. If the church’s web filter blocks the download, allow the relevant site or turn captions on at home first. After the model is downloaded, recognising a local preacher works without the internet. A web stream still needs its own connection.

    When it says Listening, speak and the words appear on the screen and in the panel. Fast shows changing words while someone is talking. More accurate waits for a short piece of speech before showing it: in one test on this computer, its first words appeared 7.8 seconds after speech began. Fast showed a provisional fragment after 1.2 seconds in the same test. The delay can differ on another computer. The words clear after a few seconds of quiet. Turn captions off to change modes.

    The best sound

    Captions are only as good as the sound they hear.

    • Best: a cable from the sound desk (a spare aux or record output) into the computer: into its microphone socket, or a USB sound adaptor, which costs a few pounds. The words come out clean, without the room's echo and the congregation's coughs.
    • All right: a USB microphone near the preacher, or on the lectern.
    • Not good: the laptop's own microphone at the back of the room. It hears the whole room.

    Another church’s stream

    Add the parent church’s YouTube or Vimeo link to the service and put its video on the congregation screen. Under Sound from, choose Video playing on congregation screen, then turn captions on. PraiseAV listens directly to that screen’s sound, without a cable or a room microphone. The video remains audible. Captions appear in a separate strip below the player, so they do not cover it.

    Test the actual stream before the service. Internet is needed for the video, even after the caption model has been downloaded. Advertisements and any other sound in the video may also be captioned. If the stream moves from speech to a hymn or other music, turn captions Off and turn them back On when speaking resumes; sung words can come out garbled. For a local preacher, switch Sound from back to Microphone or sound desk.

    Songs

    While a PraiseAV song item is on the screen, captions pause: the song's words are already there, and singing doesn't caption well. This automatic pause does not detect songs inside a web stream. To caption during PraiseAV songs too (say, for a solo whose words aren't on screen), tick Show during songs too.

    Size and place

    Choose Small, Normal or Large. The captions sit in a dark strip along the bottom of the congregation screen, above any parent alerts, and the slide's words move up to make room.

    What to expect

    Both modes can mishear names, Bible places, unusual words, and overlapping speakers. More accurate reduced mistakes in our recorded sermon and audiobook tests, but it does not promise a verbatim transcript. Rehearse with the actual sound desk and preacher before relying on it during a service.

    More accurate uses more processing power. If its words fall further and further behind on the church computer, switch to Fast or use a stronger computer.

    Credits

    Fast uses NVIDIA's NeMo streaming FastConformer English model (CC BY 4.0). More accurate uses OpenAI's Whisper small English model (MIT), converted to ONNX by the sherpa-onnx project. Both run with the free sherpa-onnx toolkit (Apache 2.0).
