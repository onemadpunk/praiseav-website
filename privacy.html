Privacy policy

Last updated 1 November 2026 · PraiseAV Windows and optional PraiseAV Suite

The short version
  • PraiseAV for Windows has no account, no tracking, no analytics and no adverts.
  • Your songs, services, media and settings stay on your computer, or in a folder you choose (OneDrive, Google Drive or a USB stick).
  • We (the people who make PraiseAV) don’t receive your songs, services, media or anything you put on screen.
  • PraiseAV only uses the internet for the things listed below, and mostly only when you ask it to.

Who we are

PraiseAV is free presentation software for churches, made by OneMadPunk, an independent developer in the United Kingdom. In this policy “we” means OneMadPunk, and “you” means the church or person using PraiseAV.

What PraiseAV keeps on your computer

PraiseAV stores these on the computer it’s installed on. None of it is sent to us.

  • Your song library: words, authors, copyright and CCLI numbers, and any chords or backing tracks you add.
  • Song usage: which songs were shown, printed or streamed and when, so you can make your CCLI report. It stays on your computer; PraiseAV doesn’t send reports to CCLI or anyone else.
  • Services you save, media you add (pictures, videos, music, slides) and settings such as themes, your church’s name and logo, congregation-alert group names and screen setup.
  • Backups: a weekly automatic backup of songs, settings and services in your Documents folder (the last 8 are kept), and any backups you make yourself.
  • Phone remote: if you use it, the names of the phones and tablets you allow, and a scrambled key for each (the key itself is only on that device).
  • Recordings you make with ● Record or while going live: video files in the recordings folder you choose (Documents › PraiseAV Recordings unless you pick another). They’re copied to a USB stick only when you ask.
  • Your stream key, if you go live: kept encrypted with Windows’ own protection, and only ever sent to the streaming service you chose (see below).
  • Small logs to help fix problems: messages to and from your ATEM video switcher if you connect one, and how a livestream connected (never the stream key).
  • Problem reports, only if you make one with Report a problem…: a text file in Documents › PraiseAV reports with what you wrote, the Windows and PraiseAV versions, the processor and memory, the screens and what each one does, which features are on (and how many phones or cameras, not their names), recent errors, and the ATEM and livestream logs. It never includes songs, services, names or the stream key, and the Windows user folder and name are hidden. You can read all of it first, and it’s only sent if you send the email or the file yourself.
  • Phone cameras and sound timing: the names you give phone cameras, and how much to hold the sound back for each camera.

Congregation alerts are never saved. A child’s number or name you put on screen is only kept while the alert is showing, and is gone once it’s cleared. Only the group names (for example “Crèche”) and the wording are remembered. Alerts are for the people in the room: they’re left out of ◉ Go live and ● Record, and never sent to the network screens or the Livestream screen. (A camera pointed at the screen, or another program recording the whole computer screen, will see them, as the room does.)

You can delete any of this at any time: delete songs, services or media in PraiseAV, or remove PraiseAV and its folders from your computer.

When PraiseAV uses the internet

What When What’s shared
Checking for updates Only the version downloaded from praiseav.com, now and then while it’s open. The Microsoft Store version is updated by the Store instead. A request to praiseav.com for the latest version number. Like any website, the server sees your computer’s internet address and which version is asked for. We don’t use this to identify anyone, and don’t combine it with anything else.
YouTube, Vimeo and web pages you show Only when you add a YouTube or Vimeo link for live video, or a web page with + Web page or YouTube. The video or page is loaded from that website, which may set cookies and see your internet address, as it would in a browser. That website’s own privacy policy applies, for example Google (YouTube) and Vimeo.
Speech recognition for live captions Once for each caption mode you use, the first time you turn that mode on. Fast captions download a speech model (about 100 MB) from GitHub. The optional More accurate mode downloads a different model (about 375 MB) from Hugging Face. The download service sees your internet address, as with any download. See GitHub’s privacy statement and Hugging Face’s privacy policy. After the selected model is downloaded, recognition works without the internet; your audio and caption words are processed on this computer and are not sent to either service.
bible.com Only when you open “NIV and more from bible.com”. bible.com opens in its own window, as it would in a browser, and YouVersion’s privacy policy applies. It’s kept separate from the rest of PraiseAV. While that window is open, PraiseAV looks at verses you copy (see “The clipboard” below); it never reads the page itself.
SongSelect Only when you open “From SongSelect”. SongSelect (songselect.ccli.com) opens in its own window, as it would in a browser, and you sign in there with your church’s own CCLI account. CCLI’s privacy policy applies. Your password is typed into CCLI’s page, never into PraiseAV, and the sign-in is kept on this computer in that window only, separate from the rest of PraiseAV. PraiseAV never reads or clicks anything on the page; when you press SongSelect’s Download, it adds that song to your library. Nothing is sent to us.
Going live Only while you’re livestreaming with ◉ Go live. What’s on the congregation screen and the sound you chose are sent, with your stream key, to the service you picked (YouTube, Facebook, or another address you enter). That service’s own privacy policy applies: Google (YouTube), Meta (Facebook). Nothing is sent to us.
Links that open your browser When you click one, for example to download LibreOffice. Your normal web browser opens the page; that website’s own policy applies.

On your church’s own network only: the phone remote (a page served from the PraiseAV computer to phones on the same Wi-Fi, after you allow each one), phone cameras (a phone’s camera picture sent over the Wi-Fi to the PraiseAV computer), screens on the network (the words or the congregation screen as web pages, only while you have them turned on), and an ATEM video switcher. PraiseAV limits these pages to devices with local or private network addresses; don’t forward their ports from your router to the internet. The phone remote and phone camera use HTTPS with a certificate made on the PraiseAV computer. A phone may ask you to accept that local certificate before opening either page. Network screens and the words-on-phones page use HTTP on your local network. Previously allowed remote devices must pair again after this change.

Folders you choose to sync

If you use a church folder in OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox (services, songs, media, the church setup and a copy of the weekly backup), or keep services or backups there, those files are synced by Microsoft, Google or Dropbox under their own privacy policies, using the account you’re signed in to on that computer. PraiseAV just saves files in the folder you chose; it doesn’t connect to those services itself, and never signs in to them.

Camera, microphone and screen

If you choose a camera, capture device or phone camera for live video, PraiseAV shows its picture on your screens. If you choose a sound input (such as the sound desk), PraiseAV uses it for recordings and livestreams. The congregation screen is only recorded when you press ● Record (or go live with “Also save a recording” ticked), and only sent anywhere while you’re going live. Windows shows when the camera or microphone is in use.

If you turn on live captions, PraiseAV listens to the microphone or sound input you choose and uses local speech recognition to turn speech into words on this computer. Recognition can make mistakes, especially with names, accents or background sound. What’s said is never sent anywhere and isn’t saved: the words are shown on screen and then cleared.

Background music (Spotify and other music apps)

If you use the music controls in PraiseAV’s top bar, PraiseAV works the music app on the same computer through Windows’ own media controls: play, pause, next, its volume and which speakers it plays through. It reads the song’s title to show it. PraiseAV doesn’t connect to Spotify or sign in to anything; the music app’s own privacy policy applies to the app itself.

The clipboard

When you press “Paste verses”, or while the bible.com window is open, PraiseAV reads what you’ve copied to find Bible verses. This stays on your computer. Nothing on the clipboard is sent anywhere.

The Microsoft Store

If you install PraiseAV from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft handles the download and updates under the Microsoft Privacy Statement. Depending on your Windows settings, Microsoft may give us anonymous, combined figures such as how many times PraiseAV has been installed and reports of crashes, which help us fix problems. These don’t include your songs, services or anything you put on screen.

Optional PraiseAV Suite web companion

PraiseAV Suite is a separate web app. In church mode it uses the Firebase project that the church administrator creates and owns. People sign in with their own Google account. Firebase Authentication processes their account identifier, email and display name; Google's privacy policy applies. An administrator approves and assigns roles before church records can be read.

Cloud Firestore in the church's project stores the records that authorised users enter: service plans, songs, people and pastoral notes, child check-ins and medical notes, groups, room bookings, and giving records. The church chooses who may see each area through the Suite's roles and its Firestore rules. These records are hosted by Google in the location the church chose. The people who make PraiseAV do not receive a copy by default. Contact the church administrator about a record concerning you or your child.

The browser stores the chosen project connection details, which are not an administrator password. An older Suite version stored records in that browser's localStorage; the upgraded Suite can read them for backup but does not upload them automatically. Sample mode keeps example data in memory. Downloaded .praiseav service files and JSON backups are kept wherever the person saves them; JSON backups are not encrypted and can contain sensitive personal information.

The Suite does not send live projector commands or public parent alerts. A parent page uses a public pager reference separate from the private child pickup code. The Suite has no payment processor; giving records are entered by the church. See the Suite roles and privacy guide.

Children

PraiseAV Windows is used by churches and isn’t directed at children. Its congregation alerts are shown on screen and not saved (see above). The optional Suite can store child check-in records in the church's Firebase project when authorised church staff enter them.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law (UK GDPR) you have rights over personal information held about you. PraiseAV Windows keeps information on the church's computer or chosen folders. Suite church records are held in the church's Firebase project; ask the church administrator about access, correction or deletion of those records. We do not receive them by default. If you contact us, we’ll use your message only to reply, and delete it when it’s no longer needed. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

Changes to this policy

If PraiseAV starts using information differently, we’ll update this page and the date at the top before the new version is released. PraiseAV will never start sending your songs, services or media to us without asking you first.
